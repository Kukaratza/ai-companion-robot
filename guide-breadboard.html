1Meet your breadboard

A breadboard is a board full of little holes. You push parts and wires into the holes. No glue, no soldering. But underneath, some holes are secretly joined by metal. Point at a hole (or tap it) to find out which ones!

👆 Point at any hole!
🧠 RememberHoles in the same column on the same side of the middle groove are joined. The long rails are joined all the way along. (On the big BB-102 board, each rail is cut in half in the middle, so power only travels along one half.)

2The 5 golden rules

1️⃣
Columns are joined. Holes a b c d e in one column are one wire. Holes f g h i j are another.
2️⃣
The groove is a wall. The top half and bottom half are NOT joined. That's why chips straddle the groove.
3️⃣
Rails run far. The red and blue lines are joined along their whole length. We use the blue one as the ground highway.
4️⃣
One pin, one column. Two different pins must never share a column, or they get stuck together (a short circuit).
5️⃣
Unplug first! Pull out the USB cable before you move any wire.
🧩
To connect two things: put the ends in different parts of the board, and join them with a wire. Never put two pins side-by-side in the same column.

3The classic mistake 😱

Here are two wires. One carries the GPIO9 signal and the other carries GPIO10. Switch between the wrong way and the right way.

🔎 Do a "strip check"Before plugging in, look at every column that has a wire or pin. Ask: "Is there exactly one job in this column?" Side-by-side columns are fine, because each column is its own strip.

👩‍👧 Good to knowThis picture matches the common ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 layout (pin names 3V3 … 5V G along one edge). Always trust the labels printed on your own parts. Pin order on the mic and amplifier can be different between brands. Column numbers here are counted from the left edge of the picture. The picture shows the Makeronics BB-102 breadboard: two long strips with four power rails, and every rail is cut in the middle (between column 30 and 31), so all the power wires here stay in columns 1 to 30. The brain sits on strip 1 and the other parts on strip 2. If your breadboard is different, the same ideas work, but the column numbers will change. You don't need a power module: the USB cable powers everything through the ESP32.

    5Wiring checklist

    Every wire in one place. Tick each one off as you plug it in. Wires are matched by name, like a matching game!

    ⚠️ Safety first!Always unplug the USB cable before changing any wire. Ask a grown-up to check your wires before you plug in. A wire in the wrong place can break a part.
    🙌 Only one row of the brain's pins is used!On your breadboard the pins on one side have no free holes, so everything plugs into the row next to the free holes. In Breadboard Buddy's picture the free holes are at the bottom and that row reads 3V3, 3V3, RST, 4, 5, 6, 7, 15, 16, 17, 18, 8, 3, 46, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 5V, G (USB ports on the right). If your free holes are on the other side, the same row reads backwards: G, 5V, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 46, 3, 8, 18, 17, 16, 15, 7, 6, 5, 4, RST, 3V3, 3V3. The brain is on strip 1. The screen, microphone and amplifier are on strip 2.
